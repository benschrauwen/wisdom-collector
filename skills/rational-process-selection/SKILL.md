# Rational process selection

Every productive analytical activity starts with a question. Match the question to the right process before doing any analysis. Using the wrong process wastes effort and produces answers nobody asked for.

---

## The four rational processes

| Core question | Thinking pattern | Process to invoke |
|---|---|---|
| **"What's going on?"** | Assessing and clarifying | **Situation Appraisal** |
| **"Why did this happen?"** | Cause-and-effect | **Problem Analysis** |
| **"Which course of action?"** | Choice-making | **Decision Analysis** |
| **"What lies ahead?"** | Future anticipation | **Potential Problem / Opportunity Analysis** |

> **Rule:** Identify the question first, then invoke the matching process. Mixing question types inside a single analysis session is the most common source of circular discussion.

---

## Critical distinction: Problem vs. Decision

These two are the most frequently confused.

| Situation | Right process |
|---|---|
| Gap between expected and actual performance, **cause unknown** | Problem Analysis — answer "Why?" |
| Cause is known; you need to choose among options | Decision Analysis — answer "Which?" or "How?" |

If someone says "we have a problem" but immediately proposes solutions, they have skipped Problem Analysis. Push back: *"Do we know the cause?"* If not, the cause must be found before solutions are evaluated.

---

## Situation Appraisal: when to use it first

Use Situation Appraisal when you face a messy cluster of issues and cannot yet tell which process applies. Its job is to:

1. Separate distinct concerns from the tangle.
2. Clarify what is actually at stake in each concern.
3. Prioritize which concern to address first.
4. Assign each concern to the correct process (Problem Analysis, Decision Analysis, or PPOA).

Situation Appraisal is the entry point when no one agrees on what the real issue is.

---

## Potential Problem / Opportunity Analysis: when to use it

Invoke this process when a decision has been made or a plan is being built and you need to ask: *"What could go wrong — or go especially right — from here?"* It is forward-looking; the other three processes are reactive or evaluative.

---

## Building a team around shared process

Effective teams are not built on interpersonal chemistry. They are built on **shared processes + shared language**. Camaraderie is a *result* of successfully working through real problems together, not its precondition.

- Introduce common analytical processes and vocabulary first.
- Apply them on real, high-stakes issues immediately.
- Cohesion follows from shared success.

---

## Conditions for durable adoption of rational processes

When introducing these processes to a team or organization, all three conditions must be present or adoption will stall:

1. **Perceived benefit** — people must see the process as being in their own interests, not just management's.
2. **Means for fulfillment** — tools, training, and time to actually use the process must be provided.
3. **Visible payoff and recognition** — results from using the process must be acknowledged explicitly and publicly.

**Implementation sequence that works:**
- The senior sponsor uses the process visibly on real issues first — credibility requires visible personal use.
- Cascade from senior team → next layer → frontline managers within a short window (weeks, not months).
- Modify existing systems and procedures to institutionalize use.
- Recognize and reward results explicitly.

> If any of the three conditions is missing, the change will not stick regardless of training quality.

---

## Anti-patterns

- **Mixing processes** — running Decision Analysis while the cause is still unknown; running Problem Analysis when the cause is already understood.
- **Skipping Situation Appraisal** — diving into Problem Analysis on a tangled situation before separating distinct concerns; this produces solutions to the wrong problem.
- **Treating team-building activities as a prerequisite** — cohesion without shared process does not produce effective teams.
- **Sponsoring adoption without visible personal use** — mandating a process the sponsor is not seen using kills credibility immediately.
